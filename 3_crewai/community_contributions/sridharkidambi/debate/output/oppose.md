I oppose the motion that the protest is right.

While concerns about NEET, leaks, and stress are understandable, **protesting for a ban is the wrong response**. What students need is **reform, not rejection**. NEET was created to bring **one common, merit-based standard** across the country so that admissions are not dominated by local favoritism, multiple entrance systems, or inconsistent state-level standards. Scrapping it would not solve inequality — it would likely **increase confusion, corruption, and uneven access**.

If the real issue is paper leaks, unfair competition, or coaching bias, then the answer is **stronger security, stricter enforcement, better scholarship support, and more transparent administration**. Abolishing the exam punishes the system by destroying a national benchmark that, in principle, gives every student one opportunity under the same rules. That is especially important for students from smaller towns who benefit from a single standardized exam rather than a fragmented admissions process.

Realistically, protests should be judged by whether their demand improves the system. A blanket “ban NEET” demand does not. It risks replacing one flawed process with **many more flawed processes**. The better path is to fix NEET, not force its removal.

So, the protest is **wrong in its central demand** because it attacks the tool instead of correcting the problem.
